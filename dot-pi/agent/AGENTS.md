- Never add AI attribution or `Co-authored-by` trailers.

- Link whatever you refer to, whenever a link exists: issues, PRs, comments, CI runs, docs, pages and vault notes. Do it in every message, including repeat mentions and short follow-ups, and say in a few words what the thing is. Don't coin short codes, numbered tags or abbreviations for work items, agents, sessions, panes, files or branches (`w464`, `lane B`, `probe 3`): call each thing what it is in the project's language, such as "the gateway address-ownership test" or "the orchestrator". An opaque identifier that already exists, such as a session ID, a commit hash or a block ID, goes beside the plain name when Bryan needs it to act, never in place of it. If the project has no word for a thing yet, describe it in plain words and ask Bryan what to call it.
- When Bryan asks for ideas, or how something could be done, answer with the range of shapes before any single design. Give three to five that really differ in what runs where, what gets built versus adopted, and what he gives up, not variants of one. Always include the least machinery that could do the job (try deleting half of the obvious design) and anything already built. Put them in a table of what he'd run and the catch, then name the one you'd pick and why. Don't lead with the conventional architecture and elaborate it, and don't answer pushback with just one more alternative.
